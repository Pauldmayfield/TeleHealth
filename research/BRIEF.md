# Access TeleCare research brief
Researched 2026-11-02. Merged from four working files in this folder (`part1_` to `part4_`), which carry the full source lists and every claim's tag.

**How to read the tags**
- **REPORTED**: stated on a page that was opened and read (filing, company page, press release, study).
- **ESTIMATED**: analyst or third-party figure, or my inference or arithmetic.
- **UNVERIFIED**: seen only in a search snippet or secondary site, or could not be confirmed.
- **Company-reported** means a marketing or press claim by Access TeleCare with no independent check.

**Read this first**
1. After the 2022 take-private, **Access TeleCare has disclosed no revenue, EBITDA, margin or debt figures.** The last audited numbers are FY2021, when it was SOC Telemed. Every current-year figure you will hear is either company marketing or inference.
2. **I found no public source for a "$300M" revenue target.** The only $300M I found is the roughly $300M estimated deal value in 2022. If "path to $300M" came from your interview materials, treat it as inside information you cannot cite.
3. **Pricing is not public for anyone.** No vendor publishes per-consult or monthly rates.
4. **The "$15,000 retained revenue per avoided transfer" figure is an industry heuristic, not independent evidence.** It appears in an Avel press release and a conference abstract and nowhere else. Use it with that label.
5. **Medicare telehealth flexibilities are extended through Dec 31, 2027.** The next hard policy dates are the DEA prescribing flexibilities (Dec 31, 2026) and the 2027 physician fee schedule cut.
6. Several key pages blocked fetching (BusinessWire, Becker's, D Magazine, Endpoints, PitchBook). Where a claim rests only on a snippet, it is marked UNVERIFIED.

## 4. The market and its economics

Full detail with all source links: [part4_market_policy.md](part4_market_policy.md).

**Size and growth** (all ESTIMATED; paid analyst reports, global, definitions differ):
- **Telestroke ~$2.0-2.2B in 2025, ~12% CAGR.** Three firms cluster there ([Roots](https://www.rootsanalysis.com/reports/telestroke-services-market.html): $2.18B and 12.3%). TechSci is the high-base, low-growth outlier ($2.94B, 8%).
- **Tele-ICU $4-9B**, depending on whether hardware is included. IMARC says $7.3B including hardware, software and services ([page](https://imarcgroup.com/tele-intensive-care-unit-market)). Services-only figures near $4-5B are closer to what a vendor sells.
- **Telepsychiatry $11.5-26.5B** (snippets) mixes consumer outpatient with hospital coverage. It is **not meaningful for acute care.**
- No independent figure exists for US hospital tele-specialty contract revenue.

**Specialist shortage**
- **AAMC (Mar 2024):** 13,500 to 86,000 total physician shortage by 2036. For medical specialties the range runs from a 5,500 shortage to a 3,700 *surplus*, and critical care and pulmonology are named as a possible surplus ([PDF](https://www.aamc.org/media/75231/download), REPORTED). **Critical care is a maldistribution story, not a headcount story, and that is a useful counterpoint for the tele-ICU pitch.** The report has no stand-alone neurology or psychiatry numbers in the text I extracted.
- **Neurology:** the AAN-funded study found 10 neurologists per 100,000 in the lowest-access areas against 43 in the highest; 21% of rural stroke patients saw a neurologist versus 31% urban ([AAN](https://www.aan.com/PressRoom/Home/PressRelease/3843)). A 2026 *Headache* paper found 66.3% of US counties have no neurologist ([NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/national-analysis-reveals-certified-headache-specialists-absent-more-than-90-us-counties)).
- **Psychiatry:** a May 2026 HRSA-model study projects adult psychiatrist supply falling 12.3% by 2037, with adequacy dropping from 70% to 43% (UNVERIFIED, from a Becker's snippet: [link](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/?p=10988)).
- **Not found:** a current national intensivist or neurologist projection.

**Rural hospital finances**
- **Chartis 2026 ([HFMA recap](https://www.hfma.org/fast-finance/rural-hospital-operating-margins-2025/)):** the share with negative operating margin was 50% in 2023, 46% in 2024 and **41.2% in 2025**; **417 are vulnerable to closure**; 206 have closed or converted since 2010; 331 stopped offering obstetrics from 2011 to 2024 ([slides](https://info.chartis.com/hubfs/CCRH/Policy%20Institute%202026/Chartis_Rural%20Safety%20Net_2026%20Policy%20Institute%20Slides%2002.09.26%20FNL.pdf)). The earlier Chartis report said 432 vulnerable; it covers a different data year, so use 2026.
- **Sheps Center (fetched):** 197 closures or conversions since 2005 and 154 since 2010; **56 Rural Emergency Hospitals** ([closures](https://www.shepscenter.unc.edu/programs-projects/rural-health/rural-hospital-closures/), [REH](https://www.shepscenter.unc.edu/programs-projects/rural-health/rural-emergency-hospitals/)). Chartis and Sheps differ on the post-2010 count because they treat REH conversions differently. Quote the source with the number.
- Advocacy-group risk lists (CHQPR ~700+) use broader definitions and are UNVERIFIED. Chartis's 417 is the more conservative figure.
- **Medicaid cuts and RHTP:** the 2025 reconciliation law (OBBBA) cuts rural Medicaid. KFF's estimate is ~$137B over 10 years, secondhand and UNVERIFIED. Work requirements start around Jan 1, 2027. The **Rural Health Transformation Program is $50B over FY2026-2030**, with all 50 states awarded Dec 29, 2025 and year-one awards of $147M to $281M ([HC Innovation](https://www.hcinnovationgroup.com/policy-value-based-care/health-equity/news/55340817/cms-breaks-out-state-level-rural-health-transformation-program-funding)). Chartis says RHTP is unlikely to offset the Medicaid cuts ([Chartis](https://www.chartis.com/insights/obbbas-early-shockwaves-rural-closures-rate-cuts-and-preemptive-layoffs)). **That is a sales tailwind and a customer-margin headwind at once.** Whether any state plan funds hospital tele-specialty subscriptions: not verified.

**Reimbursement rules that matter (status as of 2026-11-02)**
- **Medicare telehealth flexibilities:**
  - They lapsed Oct 1, 2025 in the shutdown and were restored by the Nov 12, 2025 law through Jan 30, 2026. They lapsed again Jan 31, 2026.
  - The **Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, signed Feb 3, 2026, extends them through Dec 31, 2027**, and hospital-at-home through Sept 30, 2030 ([AASM](https://aasm.org/congress-ends-partial-government-shutdown-and-extends-telehealth-flexibilities-and-the-work-gpci-floor-through-2027/), [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/medicare-telehealth-waivers-extended-for-several-more-years)). The exact signing date is snippet-level.
  - Permanent legislation (the CONNECT for Health Act) has not passed.
  - **Not verified:** whether the Jan 31 to Feb 3 gap was paid retroactively, and FY2027 funding status.
- **Other dates:**
  - **Dec 31, 2026:** DEA telemedicine prescribing flexibilities expire. A final special-registration rule went to the White House Aug 25, 2026 and its publication is unconfirmed ([Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/?p=60906)). This matters for telepsychiatry.
  - **Jan 1, 2027:** work GPCI floor ends.
  - **2027 fee schedule (proposed):** conversion factor falls 1.2% to 1.7% as the one-year 2.5% bump expires ([CMS fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2027-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-proposed-rule)). **I found no final rule as of today.**
  - **2028:** in-person requirement for tele-mental health.
- **Telestroke is the exception.** A 2018 law removed the geographic and originating-site-type limits for acute stroke telehealth from Jan 1, 2019, so telestroke does not depend on the 2027 cliff. I read this only from search summaries, since the CMS PDF would not parse ([Noridian](https://med.noridianmedicare.com/web/jfa/topics/modifiers/g0)).
- **Frequency limits** on inpatient, SNF and critical-care telehealth consults were permanently removed in the 2026 fee schedule, which helps tele-ICU and inpatient neuro ([AASM](https://aasm.org/aasm-analysis-of-the-2026-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule/)).
- **How the vendor model is paid:** the hospital pays the vendor a monthly subscription, and the remote specialist usually bills a separate professional claim. REPORTED: [Health Affairs study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9188431).
- **My analysis, not sourced (ESTIMATED):** the core transaction, a hospital paying a vendor, does not depend on Medicare telehealth rules. The exposure is in non-stroke services at non-rural sites and in telepsychiatry prescribing. I did not verify Access's urban client share or its billing structure.

A different tracker (Manatt) counts 23 with caveats, because it defines parity differently.

**Licensure and credentialing**
- **Interstate Medical Licensure Compact:** 46 jurisdictions (44 states plus DC and Guam), 220,000+ licenses, per the compact's Aug 2026 presentation ([PDF](https://ombc.ca.gov/activity/20260825_mat_9.pdf)). Older sources say 42 or 43; use 44. Tele-neurologists still need per-state licenses where states are not members.
- **PSYPACT** covers psychologists, not psychiatrists. About 41 states, with a count that has risen since (UNVERIFIED; the official site blocked).
- **Credentialing by proxy** is allowed under CMS rules effective July 2011 (42 CFR 482.22). A hospital may rely on the distant-site hospital's or telemedicine entity's credentialing through a written agreement, provided the clinician is licensed in the hospital's state ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2011-05-05/html/2011-10875.htm), REPORTED).

**Transferred patient versus retained admission**
- **Averted transfers:** in 4,324 tele-ED cases at 65 hospitals, ~20% were averted transfers, with **$2,673 of avoidable transport cost per patient**; 63.6% of savings went to public insurance ([Rural Health Research Gateway](https://www.ruralhealthresearch.org/publications/1370), REPORTED).
- **West Virginia telestroke abstract (May 2026):** post-thrombolysis transfers fell 57%; the authors estimate ~$3,000 direct savings per averted transfer and **~$15,000 revenue per retained patient**. It is a conference abstract, not peer reviewed ([link](https://www.synapsesocial.com/papers/69fd7f0dbfa21ec5bbf0767d)).
- **Avel press release (June 2025)** claims the same ~$15,000 per avoided transfer with no method or sample ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/avel-ecare-positions-technology-lifeline-hospital-viability-and-patient-care)). **The two matching figures look like a shared industry heuristic, not independent confirmation.**
- **Counter-evidence:** a VA telestroke evaluation found +$4,821 in 30-day cost and no 12-month savings (UNVERIFIED snippet: [link](https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/medical-imaging/computed-tomography-ct/va-telestroke-program-prevents-unnecessary-hospital)). Tele-ICU meta-analyses show lower ICU mortality but no significant hospital mortality or length-of-stay effect, and are all before-after designs ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3580710/), UNVERIFIED).
- **Air ambulance:** median cost per transport $10,199 to $13,017 in a 2017 industry-funded study (UNVERIFIED, dated).
- **Not found:** any payer-side study of Medicare Advantage or commercial cost per transferred patient. Who benefits from retention depends on payer mix.
