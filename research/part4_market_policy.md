## (B) Specialist shortage
- AAMC (March 2024; read PDF text): total projected shortage 13,500 to 86,000 physicians by 2036; primary care 20,200-40,400; medical specialties from 5,500 shortage to 3,700 SURPLUS, with critical care/pulmonology named as a potential surplus specialty; "Other specialties" (includes neurology, psychiatry) 19,500 shortage to 4,300 surplus. REPORTED. https://www.aamc.org/media/75231/download . The report does not publish stand-alone neurology/psychiatry numbers in the text I could extract. Critical-care "surplus" is a notable counterpoint to the shortage narrative for tele-ICU pitch (maldistribution, not headcount).
- Neurology access (AAN-funded Medicare study, Neurology Dec 2020): areas with fewest neurologists 10 per 100k vs 43 per 100k; stroke patients seeing a neurologist 21% rural vs 31% urban. REPORTED. https://www.aan.com/PressRoom/Home/PressRelease/3843

UNVERIFIED (Becker's page returned 403; figures from search snippet). https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/?p=10988

Do Medicare flexibilities matter for that model? My analysis (ESTIMATED, not sourced): (1) The core transaction, hospital paying vendor, is a contract expense paid from DRG/OPPS/ED revenue and is not conditional on Medicare telehealth rules. (2) Pro-fee collection matters to the vendor only where the vendor bills; its exposure to flexibilities is on non-stroke services (tele-ICU, inpatient neuro, psychiatry) delivered to patients at non-rural or non-eligible sites. Rural hospital/CAH patients generally satisfy the statutory originating-site/geography test even without flexibilities, but I did not verify the share of Access TeleCare clients that are urban. (3) Medicare is only part of payer mix; Medicaid covers live video in all 50 states + DC, home as originating site in 48 (CCHP Fall 2025). (4) Highest-risk items are DEA prescribing (Dec 31, 2026), telepsychiatry in-person rule (2028), and new platform/incident-to modifiers that increase audit visibility. Verify with Access TeleCare billing structure.

## (F) Cost of transfer vs retained admission
- Averted-transfer data (Telemedicine and e-Health 2020, 4,324 tele-ED cases, 65 hospitals, six networks): ~20% of cases were averted transfers; 43% of those discharged routinely; $2,673 avoidable transport cost per patient; 63.6% of savings accrue to public insurance. REPORTED. https://www.ruralhealthresearch.org/publications/1370
- West Virginia telestroke hub-and-spoke (ESJ conference abstract, May 2026): post-thrombolysis transfers fell from avg 143.7/yr to 62 in 2025 (-56.8%); 297 retained patients, sICH 0.34%; authors estimate ~$3,000 direct savings per averted transfer (mostly air ambulance) and ~$15,000 revenue per retained patient. REPORTED but author estimates, conference abstract, not peer reviewed. https://www.synapsesocial.com/papers/69fd7f0dbfa21ec5bbf0767d
- Avel eCare (June 2025 press release): 1.75 fewer ICU days ($5,688), 1.55 fewer hospital days ($3,548/admission), 13 fewer vent hours, ~$15,000 retained revenue per avoided transfer; no method, sample or comparator given. REPORTED claim, vendor marketing. https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/avel-ecare-positions-technology-lifeline-hospital-viability-and-patient-care . Note the $15k figure matches the WV abstract exactly: it may be a shared industry heuristic, not independent confirmation.
- VA telestroke (Value in Health 2022, snippet): +$4,821 30-day cost, no 12-month savings; transfers fell at 9 of 21 sites. UNVERIFIED. https://radiologybusiness.com/topics/medical-imaging/computed-tomography-ct/va-telestroke-program-prevents-unnecessary-hospital . Hub-and-spoke model (Switzer): ~$358k/yr network savings for 1 hub + 7 spokes, savings fall as transfer rate rises (snippet). UNVERIFIED. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1357633X19868097
- Air ambulance: median cost per transport $10,199 (independent) to $13,017 (hospital-based) in 2017 AAMS-commissioned study; Medicare covered ~59% of cost; older MedPAC avg Medicare payment $4,908 (2011). UNVERIFIED (snippets, dated, industry-funded). https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/?p=101395 , https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-13-6-highlights.pdf
- Tele-ICU evidence: Young 2011 meta-analysis (13 studies, 41,374 pts, all before-after): ICU mortality OR 0.80, not significant for hospital mortality or hospital LOS; later 2018 meta-analysis: no significant hospital LOS effect, cost ~$50-100k per tele-ICU bed first year; Lilly 2011 single-center mortality drop described by Kahn as implausibly large. UNVERIFIED (snippet). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3580710/
- Who pays: the receiving hub and the payer, not the sending rural hospital, bear most transfer cost; a retained patient is revenue for the rural hospital (DRG; $15k is a heuristic) but a cost to commercial/MA payers only if the community stay is cheaper than hub care; for Medicare FFS the retained admission is usually less costly than the transfer pair. I found no payer-side (MA/commercial) per-transfer cost study. Treat all "$15k" values as ESTIMATED. Retention depends on payer mix; rural hospitals' MA pays ~10% less than FFS (AHA, via NRHA snippet, UNVERIFIED); MA prior-auth delays for transfers 24h to days (snippet). https://ruralhealth.us/NationalRuralHealth/media/Documents/Advocacy/2025/Medicare-Advantage.pdf

- AI LVO detection and workflow: Viz.ai (ISC 2026, March 2026 press release; page 403, details from search snippet): single regional PSC door-in-door-out 202 -> 113 min (-44%); CTA-to-detection -84%; notification 45 -> 7 min; bundled with transfer protocols, vendor-reported; still above 120 min benchmark. UNVERIFIED. https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260305970976/en/Viz.ai-Study-Demonstrates-44-Reduction-in-Interfacility-Stroke-Transfer-Times . Multicenter study: 11.2-minute door-to-groin reduction (snippet). UNVERIFIED. Implication (inference, ESTIMATED): AI speeds up transfer of LVO patients, which supports drip-and-ship to thrombectomy centers and may reduce the role of a live neurologist for LVO routing, while tele-neurologists still needed for lysis decision, non-LVO stroke, mimics and retained patients. Viz.ai claims >2,000 US hospitals (company profile; UNVERIFIED). RapidAI: FDA clearances (NCCT stroke 2023, aortic Feb 2026); no head-to-head data found. https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230418005344/en/RapidAI-Receives-First-and-Only-FDA-510k-Clearance-of-Non-Contrast-CT-Imaging-Product-to-Accelerate-Acute-Stroke-Triage

UNVERIFIED. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8353578 . No 2026 AHA/ASA guideline change verified.
