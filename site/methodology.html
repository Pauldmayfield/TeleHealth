How this dashboard was built, and what the numbers mean

Everything here is synthetic. Larkspur TeleSpecialty and every hospital in it are fictional. No real company, hospital, patient or clinician data was used.

How the synthetic data was built

A Python script (scripts/generate_data.py, fixed seed 42) creates 24 months of data, January 2024 to December 2025, for 40 hospital accounts and 70 clinicians. "Today" is January 2026. Rerunning the script gives identical files.

  • Contracts. Each hospital pays a monthly base fee that includes a set number of encounters, plus a fee per encounter above that.
  • Cost. The main cost is clinician time, about $30,000 to $34,000 per FTE per month. Gaps covered by temporary clinicians cost 1.4 times as much.
  • Four account groups. Through 2024 all accounts look alike. From February 2025, clinicians are moved from six large, mature accounts to staff eight fast-growing ones. At the six, response time slips past 10 minutes within one to two months, satisfaction falls a month later, and volume and margin follow. Three more accounts lose volume quietly while service stays healthy. The other 23 stay steady.
  • Workforce. Clinicians work full time or part time (1.0, 0.8, 0.5 or 0.25 FTE; the 0.25 group is physicians who work about 10 hours a week alongside another job). Each holds licenses in about 10 states, and can only be assigned to hospitals in states where they are licensed. Each has a primary shift (day, evening or overnight) so 24/7 coverage can be checked.
  • Checks. scripts/validate.py confirms that assigned FTE reconciles to clinician allocations, nobody exceeds their FTE or works outside their licensed states, revenue matches to the cent, and no real company or hospital names appear.

Metric definitions

MetricHow it is calculated
Contribution marginRevenue minus direct clinician cost, before overhead.
Contribution margin %Total margin dollars divided by total revenue for the period or group. Monthly percentages are never averaged.
Encounters per FTETotal encounters divided by total assigned clinician FTE. About 55 to 65 is healthy; response time breaks above about 70.
Cost per FTEDirect cost divided by assigned FTE. It rises when gaps are covered by premium-rate backfill.
Staffed % of needAssigned FTE divided by the FTE the volume needs (encounters ÷ 60).
Response vs targetAverage response minutes minus the 10-minute contract target. Positive means late.
Volume trendEncounters divided by the average of the prior three months, minus 1.
Gross revenue retentionRevenue from accounts active a year ago, 2025 versus 2024, counting no growth.
Contract value at riskAnnual contract value of accounts in the High risk tier.
Annual contract valueThe yearly revenue a contract was sized at (2024 run rate). Actual revenue moves with volume, because hospitals pay a per-encounter fee above their included encounters. Fast-growing accounts therefore bill above contract value and shrinking ones below.
Quiet churn signalLast quarter's encounters at least 10% below the same quarter a year earlier, while response time is on target. It adds a reason to the account but does not change its risk tier.
Door-to-needleHospital-wide average minutes from a stroke patient's arrival to clot-dissolving medication, shown for hospitals with tele-neurology. It reflects the whole hospital, so read it as associated with our response time, not caused by it. The AHA goal (60 minutes for 85% of patients) is shown for reference only.
UtilizationClinician time assigned to hospitals as a share of their FTE status.

How hospitals are tiered

Performance tier (what to coach), using the latest three months. It rests on two things: clinicians assigned and response time.

  • Needs assistance: response over target in 2 or more of the last 3 months, or average response above target, or clinicians assigned below 90% of need, or under 85% of encounters within the response standard.
  • Exceptional: not needing assistance, and in addition zero months over target in the last six, average response 8.5 minutes or less, 93% or more of encounters within standard, satisfaction 8.2 or more, and staffed at 95% or more of need.
  • As expected: everything else.

Staffing position: Short below 95% of need, Heavy above 115% (extra clinicians the volume does not require), otherwise Right-sized.

Risk tier (what might leave), counts four flags: response over target in 2 of the last 3 months; latest 3-month volume 10% or more below the prior 3; latest satisfaction below 7.5; latest quarter margin 5 or more points below the same quarter a year earlier. Three or four flags is High, one or two is Watch, none is Healthy.

All thresholds live in config/thresholds.json and can be changed in one place.

Glossary

Acute care telemedicineRemote specialists treating hospitalized or ER patients through video.
EncounterOne remote consult with one patient.
FTEFull-time equivalent. 1.0 is one full-time clinician; 0.5 is half time.
DensityEncounters handled per clinician FTE. Higher density means better margin, until response time breaks.
Response time / SLAMinutes from a hospital's request to the specialist joining, and the contracted standard for it.
Door-to-needleMinutes from a stroke patient's arrival to clot-dissolving medication.
TransferMoving a patient to another hospital. Avoided transfers keep revenue local.
Call payWhat hospitals pay local specialists to be on call. Telemedicine can replace it.
Credentialing by proxyA hospital relying on the telemedicine company's review of a clinician instead of its own full review.
Critical Access HospitalA small rural hospital (25 beds or fewer) with special Medicare status.
RenewalWhen a hospital decides whether to extend its contract.
BackfillCovering a staffing gap with temporary or premium-rate clinicians.
