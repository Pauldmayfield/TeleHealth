## 1. What this project is

A portfolio dashboard that shows how an analytics executive turns operating data into a decision a CEO and CFO can act on. It uses **fully synthetic data** for a **fictional company**. The goal is a live, mobile-friendly web page that a hiring manager can open from a link and understand in under a minute.

The dashboard should look and read like a real executive tool, not a data science demo. Every page answers one question, leads with a one-sentence takeaway, and shows the evidence underneath.

## 2. Ground rules (do not break these)

1. **Fictional company only.** The company is **Larkspur TeleSpecialty** (fictional). Never use the name, logo, colors, clients, executives or figures of any real telehealth company, hospital or health system. If "Larkspur TeleSpecialty" turns out to match a real company, rename it everywhere.
2. **Fictional hospitals only.** Generate made-up hospital names (for example "Cedar Ridge Regional Medical Center"). Do not use real hospital names.
3. **All operating data is synthetic.** It is generated by `scripts/generate_data.py` from the rules in section 6. Nothing in this project comes from any real company's internal data or from any interview materials.
4. **Real-world numbers appear only as labeled context.** Public benchmarks from section 11 may appear in tooltips, footnotes or an "industry context" panel, always labeled as public data with a source link. Never blend them into the synthetic company's metrics.
5. **Every page carries a footer**: "Synthetic data for a fictional company. Built by Paul Mayfield to demonstrate executive analytics." Link to a short methodology page.
6. **No PHI, no patient-level records.** Data is at the account-month and clinician-month level only.

## 3. The business, in plain English

Larkspur TeleSpecialty sells **acute care telemedicine** to hospitals. When a small or mid-sized hospital has a stroke patient, a psychiatric crisis in the ER, or a critically ill patient overnight, it often has no specialist on site. Without coverage, the patient is stabilized and transferred to a larger hospital, sometimes by helicopter. With Larkspur, a video cart is rolled to the bedside and a remote specialist joins within minutes, reviews the chart, and directs care.

**Who wins:**
- The patient gets faster specialist care closer to home.
- The hospital keeps the admission and its revenue, avoids paying local specialists for on-call coverage, and can qualify for programs like stroke certification.
- The payer avoids the cost of a transfer.
- Larkspur spreads one specialist across many hospitals.

**How Larkspur makes money:**
- Each hospital contract has a **monthly base fee** that includes a set number of encounters, plus a **per-encounter fee** above that volume.
- The main cost is the **clinician panel**: specialists paid by FTE (full-time equivalent), not per consult.
- So **margin depends on density**. A clinician covering more encounters across more hospitals earns more margin. Too few clinicians assigned and response times slip; too many and margin falls.

**Service lines in scope:** Tele-Neurology (including telestroke), Tele-Psychiatry, Tele-Critical Care.

## 4. Audience and the questions the dashboard answers

**Primary audience:** CEO and CFO in a 15-minute review, plus a private equity board member.

**The dashboard must answer, in this order:**
1. **How is the portfolio doing?** Revenue, contribution margin, encounters, client count, and trends.
2. **Which accounts are at risk of leaving, and why?** Ranked by risk and by how soon they renew.
3. **What is driving margin?** Show that the portfolio average hides distinct account patterns.
4. **Is clinician capacity matched to demand?** Where to move capacity first.
5. **What leading indicators would have caught this earlier?** With thresholds and current status.

## 5. The story the data must tell

The synthetic data is built so the dashboard's default view reveals this story without any clicking:

> Portfolio contribution margin looks flat at about 30%, but it hides diverging account groups. Starting in early 2025, clinician capacity was pulled from several large, mature accounts to staff fast-growing accounts. At the mature accounts, response times broke the 10-minute target within one to two months, hospital satisfaction fell the following month, and encounter volume and margin declined after that. Direct cost fell much less than FTE because gaps were backfilled with premium-rate coverage. Several of these accounts renew in Q1 2026. A smaller group shows quiet churn: service looks fine, but volume is falling, which suggests the hospital is building its own coverage or testing a competitor.

The lesson the dashboard teaches: **service metrics move months before margin does.**

## 6. Synthetic data specification

### 6.1 Generator rules
- Script: `scripts/generate_data.py`, Python 3 with `pandas` and `numpy` only.
- **Fixed random seed `42`** so the data is reproducible.
- Output CSVs to `data/`. Re-running the script must produce identical files.
- Period: **24 months, January 2024 to December 2025.** Treat "today" as January 2026.
- Add realistic noise (roughly plus or minus 3 to 5% month to month) but keep the story in section 5 clearly visible.

### 6.2 Scale
- **40 hospital accounts** across 4 regions (Northeast, South, Midwest, West).
- Hospital types: Critical Access (25 beds), Community (50 to 200 beds), Regional (200 to 400 beds).
- Each account subscribes to 1 to 3 service lines. Tele-Neurology is the most common.
- **About 70 clinicians** across the three specialties.

### 6.3 Account archetypes (assign before generating)

| Archetype | Count | Behavior from Feb 2025 onward |
|---|---|---|
| **At-risk** | 6 | Assigned FTE falls about 30 to 35% over 11 months. Response time rises from about 9 to 17 to 19 minutes. Satisfaction falls from about 8.3 to 6.5 with a 1-month lag. Volume falls 15 to 30% with a 2-month lag. Cost per FTE rises about 40% due to premium backfill. **4 of the 6 renew in Jan to Mar 2026.** |
| **Growth** | 8 | Volume rises 50 to 90%. FTE rises 50 to 60%. Margin improves from density. 2 or 3 drift just over the response target in late 2025 as demand outruns staffing. |
| **Quiet churn** | 3 | Service metrics stay healthy. Volume declines 15 to 20% steadily. FTE unchanged or slightly up. Renew in mid to late 2026. |
| **Steady** | 23 | Stable volume, staffing, quality. Margin near 30%. |

In 2024, all archetypes should look similar so the divergence starting in 2025 is visible.

### 6.4 Tables

**`accounts.csv`** (one row per account)
`account_id` (H-101...), `hospital_name`, `region`, `state`, `hospital_type`, `licensed_beds`, `service_lines` (pipe separated), `contract_start`, `renewal_date`, `monthly_base_fee`, `included_encounters`, `per_encounter_fee`, `annual_contract_value`, `archetype` (hidden field for validation only; never displayed as a label)

**`account_months.csv`** (one row per account per service line per month)
`account_id`, `service_line`, `month` (YYYY-MM), `encounters`, `included_encounters` (line-level, used for the revenue rule), `assigned_fte`, `avg_response_min`, `target_response_min` (10), `sla_met_pct`, `satisfaction_score` (1 to 10), `door_to_needle_min` (Tele-Neurology only, otherwise blank), `transfers_avoided`, `revenue`, `direct_cost`

**`clinicians.csv`** (one row per clinician)
`clinician_id`, `specialty`, `home_region`, `fte_status` (0.25, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0; 0.25 = physicians who contribute about 10 hours a week), `hire_date`, `shift` (Day, Evening, Overnight, for 24/7 coverage checks), `licensed_states` (pipe separated, about 10 per clinician)

**`clinician_months.csv`** (one row per clinician per account per month)
`clinician_id`, `account_id`, `month`, `fte_allocated`, `encounters`, `utilization_pct` (the clinician's total for the month, repeated on each row), `attrition_risk_flag`. A clinician with no assignment in a month gets one row with blank `account_id` and 0 FTE.

### 6.5 Consistency rules (the data must reconcile)
- For every account, service line and month: `assigned_fte` in `account_months` **equals the sum of** `fte_allocated` in `clinician_months`.
- A clinician's `fte_allocated` across all accounts in a month cannot exceed their `fte_status`.
- Clinicians can only be allocated to accounts in states they are licensed in.
- `revenue = monthly_base_fee share + max(0, encounters - included_encounters) x per_encounter_fee` (split base fee across service lines in proportion to included encounters).
- `direct_cost = assigned_fte x monthly cost per FTE`, where cost per FTE is about $30,000 to $34,000, with a premium of 1.4x applied to any backfilled share at at-risk accounts.
- Healthy encounters per FTE: about 55 to 65 per month. Response time rises as encounters per FTE exceed about 70, or as FTE falls below need.
- `transfers_avoided` is roughly 15 to 25% of Tele-Neurology and Tele-Critical Care encounters.
- `attrition_risk_flag` is more likely at utilization below 60% or above 92%.

## 7. Metric definitions (use exactly these)

| Metric | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution margin ($) | `revenue - direct_cost` | |
| Contribution margin % | `SUM(revenue - direct_cost) / SUM(revenue)` | **Never average monthly percentages.** Always ratio of sums for any period or group. |
| Encounters per FTE | `SUM(encounters) / SUM(assigned_fte)` | Density measure |
| Cost per FTE | `SUM(direct_cost) / SUM(assigned_fte)` | Rising cost per FTE with falling FTE signals premium backfill |
| Response vs target | `avg_response_min - target_response_min` | Positive means late |
| Months over target | Count of months where `avg_response_min > target_response_min` | |
| Volume trend | `encounters / average(encounters over prior 3 months) - 1` | |
| Revenue per encounter | `SUM(revenue) / SUM(encounters)` | |
| Gross revenue retention | Revenue from accounts active a year ago, this year vs last year, excluding growth | Use for portfolio health tile |
| Contract value at risk | `SUM(annual_contract_value)` for accounts in the High risk tier | |
| Renewal window | Months from "today" (Jan 2026) to `renewal_date` | |

### Risk flags and tier
Each flag is 1 if true, else 0. Thresholds live in one config file (`config/thresholds.json`) so they can be changed in one place.
- **Service flag:** response time above target in 2 or more of the last 3 months.
- **Volume flag:** latest 3-month encounters at least 10% below the same accounts' prior 3 months.
- **Satisfaction flag:** latest satisfaction below 7.5.
- **Margin flag:** latest quarter contribution margin % down 5 or more points versus the same quarter a year earlier.

**Risk score** = sum of flags (0 to 4). **Tier:** 3 or 4 = High, 1 or 2 = Watch, 0 = Healthy.
**Priority order** within a tier: soonest renewal first, then largest contract value.

## 8. Dashboard specification

One page per question. Every page starts with a **headline sentence that states the finding**, generated from the data, not hard-coded.

**Page 1. Portfolio overview**
- KPI strip: Revenue (trailing 12 months), Contribution margin % (with change vs prior year), Encounters, Active accounts, Contract value at risk.
- Hero chart: Contribution margin % by month, one line per account group (At-risk, Growth, Quiet churn, Steady) plus a dashed portfolio total line. This chart carries the whole story.
- Headline example: "Margin held at 30%, but $X.XM of contract value renewing in Q1 is at high risk."

**Page 2. Account risk**
- Ranked table: account, region, renewal date, contract value, risk score, the four flags as icons, and a one-line reason.
- Filters: region, service line, tier.
- Clicking a row opens the account drilldown.

**Page 3. Account drilldown**
- Account picker. Small multiples for 24 months: assigned FTE, response time vs target line, satisfaction, encounters, contribution margin %.
- A vertical marker on the month FTE first dropped, so the lag pattern is visible.

**Page 4. Margin drivers**
- Margin bridge (waterfall): portfolio monthly margin January 2025 to December 2025, broken into At-risk, Growth, Quiet churn, Steady.
- Scatter: encounters per FTE (x) vs contribution margin % (y), one dot per account, colored by tier.

**Page 5. Capacity and staffing**
- Clinician utilization by region and specialty, with attrition flags.
- "Where to move capacity first": accounts with the largest FTE gap to need, matched with clinicians below 70% utilization who are licensed in that state.

**Page 6. Leading indicators**
- Three tiles with current status and threshold: response time vs target, volume trend, staffing changes within 12 months of renewal.
- A note on how many months of warning each indicator gave for the at-risk accounts.

**Page 2a. Hospital performance** (added; sits beside Account risk)
- Every hospital gets a **performance tier** (Exceptional, As expected, Needs assistance) driven by clinicians assigned versus clinicians needed (encounters / 60) and response time against target. Thresholds in `config/thresholds.json`. Also a **staffing position** (Short, Right-sized, Heavy).
- Scatter of staffing coverage against response time, ranked table, tier summary cards (response, SLA, satisfaction, margin, cost per FTE).
- Distinct from risk tier: performance says who to coach now, risk says who might leave.

**Page 5 additions (Capacity and staffing)**
- Utilization by specialty and region, utilization bands, part-time (0.25 FTE) contributors.
- Growth and new coverage: a state table (hospitals, unmet need, licensed clinicians, spare time, status Covered, Thin or Open territory) and "next licenses to add" (clinician paired with the state where a license helps most).
- 24/7 coverage: FTE by shift and home region, overnight flagged thin under 1.5 FTE.
- "Where to move capacity first" lists every hospital short of clinicians, ranked High risk first, then soonest renewal, then biggest gap, and shows the surplus FTE held by Heavy hospitals as a redeployment source.

**Additions from the case-reasoning review** (ideas only; no real names or figures)

## 9. Design rules

- **Audience-first:** plain English labels, no jargon without a tooltip. Use the glossary in section 10.
- **Takeaway titles:** each chart title states the point ("At-risk accounts lost a third of their clinicians"), not the variable ("FTE by group").
- **Color with meaning:** red for High risk or at-risk group, amber for Watch, green for Growth or Healthy, gray for Steady, navy for portfolio totals. Never rely on color alone; label lines directly.
- **Numbers:** compact format ($5.7M, 1,250, 32%). Percentages to whole numbers on tiles, one decimal in tables.
- **Mobile first:** must work at 375px wide with no horizontal scrolling. Tables collapse to cards on mobile.
- **Light and dark mode** support.
- **Accessibility:** sufficient contrast, keyboard navigation, chart data available as a table.
- **Restraint:** no 3D charts, no gauges, no more than 5 colors on one chart, no decorative graphics.

**Tableau-informed practices** (added after reviewing Tableau's dashboard best-practices guidance; the Tableau Public gallery pages could not be read, so no viz was studied)
- Each page opens with the question it answers, then a generated one-sentence answer.
- The most important view sits upper-left, with big-number tiles (value, delta, sparkline) above it.
- Two or three views per page. Page 5 is split into **Capacity** and **Licensing & 24/7**. The margin bridge is now a zero-based bar chart of the change by group, with a Net bar, on the Portfolio page.
- Highlight actions: a group selector spotlights a group across charts; hovering a hospital highlights it in both the scatter and the ranked list.
- Color spotlights: grey by default, red only for what needs attention. Shapes and labels back up color.
- Bars start at zero. Direct labels replace legends. Key moments are annotated on the charts. Filters are labeled single-select buttons.
- Fonts: IBM Plex Sans (body) and IBM Plex Sans Condensed (big numbers), loaded from Google Fonts with system fallbacks.

## 12. Technical approach

- **Stack:** Python (`pandas`, `numpy`) for data generation and aggregation. A single static `index.html` dashboard using **Chart.js** from cdnjs and plain JavaScript. No backend.
- **Data flow:** `scripts/generate_data.py` writes CSVs to `data/`. `scripts/build_metrics.py` reads them and writes `site/data/metrics.json` with every aggregate the dashboard needs. The page reads only `metrics.json`.
- **Hosting:** GitHub Pages from the `site/` folder, so the dashboard has a shareable link.

## 13. Validation checks (`scripts/validate.py` must pass before any dashboard work)

1. Seed is fixed; re-running the generator produces identical files (compare file hashes).
2. FTE reconciles: account `assigned_fte` equals the sum of clinician `fte_allocated` for every account, service line and month.
3. No clinician exceeds their `fte_status` in any month, and none are allocated outside licensed states.
4. Portfolio revenue in `metrics.json` equals the sum of `revenue` in `account_months.csv` to the cent.
5. Portfolio contribution margin % lands between 28% and 32% for every month.
6. At-risk group contribution margin % falls by at least 15 points from January 2025 to December 2025; Growth group rises by at least 5 points.
7. The risk tiering puts at least 4 of the 6 at-risk accounts in High and at least 2 of the 3 quiet churn accounts in Watch or High.
8. No real company or hospital names appear anywhere in `data/` or `site/` (check against a deny list in `config/deny_list.txt`).
9. Every page shows the synthetic data footer.

`validate.py` also runs four extra checks: A (all three performance tiers populated), B (about 10 state licenses per clinician), C (quiet churn tile catches at least 2 of 3 quiet-churn accounts), D (every high-risk, short-staffed hospital appears in capacity moves).
